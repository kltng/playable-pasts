# History Bible — The Grain Shipment

> **This game has no corpus.** It is a method exercise with invented documents. This file exists
> because every Playable Past game ships one, and because a bible that honestly records "no
> sources" is more useful than a fabricated one.

## 1. Source inventory

None. No primary source, dataset, image, or secondary work was used or consulted.

Every document shown in the game was written for it. They carry no source IDs because there is
nothing to cite, and they must never be quoted as evidence of anything.

## 2. Why invent rather than use real sources

A demonstration needs documents whose problems are unambiguous: a number that inflates over
time, two interested parties with no neutral record between them, and two accounts that look
independent but share wording.

Real archives supply all three, but rarely in ten minutes of reading and never without the
period knowledge students may not have yet. Using invented documents keeps the method in the
foreground.

The trade-off is real and is stated in the game itself: students practise the method on material
that cannot reward them for knowing the period. This is a warm-up, not a substitute for work on
real sources.

| ID | Claim | Type | Basis | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C001 | Every document, name, and quantity in the game | Fictional connective tissue | Invented for this exercise | All rounds |
| C002 | Accounts written closer to an event, for practical purposes, are generally more reliable than later narrative accounts | Corroborated interpretation | Standard source-criticism practice | Round 1 |
| C003 | Later narrative sources tend to inflate numbers | Corroborated interpretation | A widely observed tendency, stated as a habit of reading rather than a law | Round 1 |
| C004 | A claim that no possible evidence could falsify is weak, not strong | Corroborated interpretation | Ordinary standard of argument | Round 2 |
| C005 | Where all surviving accounts are interested parties, the honest answer may be that the question cannot be settled | Corroborated interpretation | Standard practice | Round 2 |
| C006 | Two accounts sharing wording are one claim, not two independent ones | Corroborated interpretation | Standard test of independence in source criticism | Round 3 |
| C007 | Records made for internal use are often more reliable, because they were not made to persuade | Modeling assumption | A rule of thumb. Internal records were falsified too; the game says so | Round 3 |
| C008 | Source criticism can be scored right or wrong | Modeling assumption | A simplification the format requires. Flagged on the Sources screen and in debrief question 5 | Scoring |

No row is *directly attested*, *derived*, *contested*, *counterfactual*, or *model recall
(unverified)*. C002–C007 are ordinary method, not findings about any particular past.

## 4. Integrity safeguards

| Safeguard | How it is handled |
|---|---|
| Synthetic-source confusion | The largest risk here. Addressed three times: a notice before play, the Sources & assumptions screen, and this file. |
| False precision | Scoring implies source criticism has right answers. Named as an assumption (C008) and put to students in debrief question 5. |
| Archive bias | Round 2 turns it into the lesson: the one disinterested record is missing, and that absence is the finding. |
| Teleology | Not applicable — no historical outcome is modelled. |
| Presentism | Documents avoid period-specific institutions, so no modern category is smuggled in. |
| Trauma as entertainment | Not applicable — a grain robbery with no casualties, chosen partly for that reason. |

## 5. Open questions

- Does scoring teach the wrong lesson about a practice that is argued rather than marked? Debrief
  question 5 raises it with students rather than resolving it.
- Would replacing round two with a genuine dispute from the instructor's own unit be strictly
  better? Probably yes. The teacher guide recommends exactly that edit.
