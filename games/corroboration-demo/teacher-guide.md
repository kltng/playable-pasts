# Teacher guide — The Grain Shipment

**One file. Ten minutes. No accounts, no wifi needed.**

## What the game argues

That the reliability of a source depends on who made it, when, for whom, and what they needed
the reader to believe — and that a well-run enquiry sometimes ends in "the evidence cannot
settle this."

The second point is the one students resist. Round two is built so that every confident answer
is wrong.

## What students do

Three rounds. Each gives them two or three short documents and one question with three answers.
They choose; the game explains why that answer holds or fails; they move on.

The three rounds escalate deliberately:

1. **Two records that look contradictory are not.** A later chronicle inflates a number, while
   two contemporary records fit together once read closely.
2. **Two interested parties, and a missing disinterested record.** The correct answer is that it
   cannot be settled.
3. **Agreement is not corroboration.** Two accounts share wording, so they are one claim, not
   two. An internal household record beats both.

## Running one session

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–3 min | **Briefing.** Say only this: every document is invented, the skill is real, and there will be a round with no right answer. Do not explain source criticism first — the game is the explanation. |
| 3–10 min | **Play.** Individually, or in pairs on one device. Pairs are better: the argument between two students is the learning. |
| 10–25 min | **Debrief.** The five questions on the ending screen. Question 2 and question 4 are the ones worth real time. |

If you have a full period, follow the debrief by handing out two conflicting sources from your
own unit and asking students to run the same enquiry with no game at all.

## Getting it to students

Post `game.html` to your LMS as a file, or email it. They download and double-click. It needs no
internet after that, stores nothing, and collects no data about them.

## If something goes wrong

Refresh the page — the game restarts cleanly and nothing is stored. If the file will not open,
it was probably saved as `game.html.txt`; rename it. If the text looks like garbled symbols, the
file was saved in the wrong encoding; download it again.

## Editing it yourself

Open `game.html` in any plain text editor. The block marked `GAME_DATA` near the top holds every
word in the game — documents, questions, answers, explanations, debrief. Change the text between
the quotation marks, keep the commas and brackets, save, and reopen.

The most valuable edit is replacing round two's documents with a genuine dispute from your own
course. The structure carries over unchanged.
