# History Bible — The Mill Fire

> **This game has no corpus.** It is a method exercise with invented documents. This file exists
> because every Playable Past game ships one, and because a bible that honestly records "no
> sources" is more useful than a fabricated one.

## 1. Source inventory

None. No primary source, dataset, image, or secondary work was used or consulted.

Every paper shown in the game was written for it. They carry no source IDs because there is
nothing to cite, and they must never be quoted as evidence of anything.

## 2. Why invent rather than use real sources

The exercise needs a set of documents that date each other cleanly, plus one that describes
an early event but was written late. Real archives supply both, but rarely in a form a student
can work through in fifteen minutes without period knowledge, and a real chronicle's lateness
is usually established by scholarship the student has not read yet.

Inventing the papers lets the internal clues carry the whole argument. The trade-off is stated
in the game: students practise the method on material that cannot reward them for knowing the
period. This is a warm-up, not a substitute for work on real sources.

The mill was chosen because a mill fire with nobody hurt lets the game turn on cause without
turning on suffering. Debrief question 5 asks students what would change if it had.

## 3. Claims ledger

| ID | Claim | Type | Basis | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C001 | Every document, name, sum, and event in the game | Fictional connective tissue | Invented for this exercise | All rounds |
| C002 | Undated documents can be ordered from what each takes for granted | Corroborated interpretation | Standard practice in diplomatic and archival work | Rounds 1–2 |
| C003 | The date of composition is distinct from the date of the events described | Corroborated interpretation | Basic source criticism | Round 2 |
| C004 | Later narrative accounts can be downstream of earlier local gossip | Corroborated interpretation | A common pattern in the transmission of memory, stated as a tendency | Rounds 2–3 |
| C005 | Two documents repeating one rumour are one claim, not two | Corroborated interpretation | Standard test of independence | Round 3 |
| C006 | Documents written for other purposes carry more weight on a question than documents written to answer it | Corroborated interpretation | Standard practice; the same principle as the household accounts in *The Grain Shipment* | Round 3 |
| C007 | A probable cause can be argued from independent documents that fit one mechanism, and should be stated as probable | Modeling assumption | The game rewards a hedged inference over both a rumour and a refusal to infer. Where that line sits is argued among historians, and debrief question 4 says so | Round 3 |
| C008 | Chronology and causation can be scored right or wrong | Modeling assumption | A simplification the format requires. Flagged on the Sources screen and in the debrief | Scoring |

No row is *directly attested*, *derived*, *contested*, *counterfactual*, or *model recall
(unverified)*. C002–C006 are ordinary method, not findings about any particular past.

## 4. Integrity safeguards

| Safeguard | How it is handled |
|---|---|
| Synthetic-source confusion | The largest risk here. Addressed three times: a notice before play, the Sources & assumptions screen, and this file. |
| False precision | Scoring implies these questions have right answers. Named as an assumption (C008) and put to students in the debrief. An ordering round scores a point only when every card is in the right place. After checking, each card is still marked right or wrong, so a student can see that a near-miss was a near-miss, but it earns no point. |
| Archive bias | Present by design: the only papers written to explain the fire are the two least reliable. The game makes that the finding. |
| Teleology | Not applicable. No historical outcome is modelled. |
| Presentism | Documents avoid period-specific institutions and prices are kept vague, so no modern category is smuggled in. |
| Trauma as entertainment | A fire with no casualties, chosen for that reason. Debrief question 5 asks students to consider the version with casualties rather than the game staging it. |

## 5. Open questions

- Round three rewards "probably". Some instructors will want a version where the honest answer
  is again "cannot be settled", to stop students learning that the middle option is always
  right. Editing the third option's `correct` flag and its explanation would do it.
- Round one was edited on 2026-09-24 because it had two valid orders: the bill and the lease
  both said "this spring", so either could come first. The bill now says it is "not yet paid"
  and that "whoever takes the mill" should be told, and the lease says "the carpenter paid for
  it". Only the order A, B, C, D now fits. In round two the petition now says "half a wheel",
  to agree with the letter in round one. All of this is still invented (C001).
- Would replacing round one with four undated documents from the instructor's own unit be
  strictly better? Probably yes. The teacher guide recommends exactly that edit.
