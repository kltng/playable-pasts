The Yang Naiwu Case

Play a real Qing homicide case in six short chapters: as the county magistrate at the inquest, as the family appealing to the capital, as the commissioner at the retrial, and as an official of the Board of Punishments reviewing the whole file. Students pick passages from the 1877 memorial, the Code and the Shenbao as evidence, and argue how a wrongful conviction was made, step by step.

The Yang Naiwu Case (楊乃武案) — Teacher Guide

Six short chapters · About 65 minutes of play · Planned for a 90-minute class · Chinese and English · Hosted online

Play it here: https://kltng.github.io/yuhang-case-web/

What this game is

In 1873 a man named Ge Pinlian died in Yuhang County, Zhejiang. The county magistrate decided he had been poisoned. His wife, Ge Bishi, and a new provincial graduate, Yang Naiwu, confessed under torture, and the officials proposed death sentences for both. The case went up and down the Qing review system for more than three years, through two appeals to the capital and a retrial, until the Board of Punishments in Beijing settled it in 1877.

Students play this case in six chapters. In most chapters they take the place of a real person in the case and face the decisions that person faced. They read short extracts from the real documents: the Board of Punishments' memorial of 1877, imperial edicts, the Great Qing Code, the forensic handbook Collected Cases of Injustice Rectified (洗冤集錄), a petition to the capital, and reports in the Shanghai newspaper Shenbao.

It is a game about procedure and evidence, not a murder mystery. There is no hidden killer to find. Students do not change history: what they decide inside a chapter only changes that chapter's ending. Then the game shows what really happened, with the source, and the next chapter starts again from the real history.

Before you use it: four practical points

  1. It needs the internet. This is a web game made with the Godot engine. It is not a single file you can hand out. The files are about 49 MB; when an agent loaded the site in September 2026, the browser downloaded about 18 MB. Students need a working connection every time they open it. If thirty students start at the same moment on a weak network, expect a slow start. Open it on the classroom network a few days before class.
  2. It needs a reasonably modern computer. Use an up-to-date browser on a laptop or desktop computer. Only Chrome has been tested (see test-ledger.md); try your students' browsers before class. The design also supports tablets held sideways, but this has not been tested. Phones are not supported.
  3. Progress is saved in the browser. On a shared lab computer the next student will see the last student's progress. The title screen has a "Clear local data" button. Students can also export a save file.
  4. Screen readers are not supported in any tested way. The game draws on a canvas, and no screen-reader test has been done. If a student needs this, plan another way for them to do the work (for example, a written exercise on the same documents).

Languages

The game is fully bilingual. One control switches the whole screen between three layers:

  • 原文 — the original classical Chinese, used only for quotations from documents;
  • 白話 — a modern Chinese version (traditional characters);
  • English — an English translation.

The title screen also has a 中文 / English switch. Students can change language at any time without losing their place. AI agents wrote and revised the English. It is still marked as a draft in the source project, and no person has signed it off.

Content note

The game warns players before the parts that need it. It mentions torture, including finger presses (拶指) used on a woman; the inquest on the body and, later, the opening of the coffin; the original sentence of death by slicing (凌遲); and insulting language about women from the official documents of the time. Nothing is shown in a bloody way. In chapter 1 there is a "Skip descriptions" option: torture and the body are then described only briefly, and grades do not change.

These minutes are the design's estimates for the required path. Nobody has timed a real class.

Every chapter can be started on its own. Each one opens with a short summary of what happened before. A student who starts at chapter 4 sees the same material as a student who played from the beginning. So you can use one chapter in a shorter class. Chapter 4 is the core of the game.

What students do

The whole game uses only five kinds of action:

  • Read case documents, passage by passage, in any of the three language layers.
  • Pick a passage from a document as a point of doubt or as evidence.
  • Present a document or piece of evidence against a statement in a witness's testimony.
  • Choose one of two to four options, each with a written consequence.
  • Fill in a form with fixed fields, chosen from options (for example, a sentencing form: person → Code article → punishment → reason).

Reading is never timed. Full documents are optional; the required path asks only for the extracts.

How each passage is labelled. The game marks every piece of text by its form — original text, paraphrase, reconstruction, or game fiction — and marks historical statements by status, such as "established", "recorded assertion" or "inference". Some statements show "Not yet checked". Point these labels out to students early. Asking "how does the game know this?" is part of the exercise.

Pictures. The portraits and scenes are imagined pictures made with AI. The game says so. No real portrait of anyone in this case is known. Illustrations can be switched off.

Grading inside the game

The game's grades are feedback for students, not a course grade. There are no accounts.

  • Each graded item is shown as Achieved, Partly achieved, or Not achieved.
  • A wrong answer gives feedback, and the student can try again. After two different wrong answers a Hint button appears. After three, a Show answer button appears (sooner if a question has fewer wrong options). Some questions can be answered only once and have no hint.
  • Grades come in two separate parts: A Procedure (did the student act as the Code and the forensic handbook required?) and B Reasoning from evidence (did they point to the right passage?). A third part, C Comparison with history, is shown but not graded. There is no total score.
  • A student can reason well and still reach a different conclusion from the historical official. That can still be "Achieved".
  • At the end of a chapter students can download their results as a JSON file or as printable text. You can ask them to hand this in.

Running one 90-minute class

The design plans a 90-minute class: about 60–65 minutes of play and about 30 minutes for the briefing and the discussion. The chapter times above add up to 65, so this is tight. If your class is shorter, use chapters 1 and 4, or chapter 4 alone.

A plan for 90 minutes:

TimeWhat happens
0–10Briefing. Show the site on the projector. Set the language. Explain the labels (original, paraphrase, reconstruction, fiction). Read the content note together.

Tips:

  • Have every student open the site before class starts. The first load is the slow one.
  • Students who finish early can open the optional documents in any chapter.
  • If a computer fails, the student can start again from any chapter on another machine.

Debrief questions

These are the questions from the game's teacher materials, in the order of the chapters. The answers are not given here.

Prologue

  1. 葛品連自己竊聽過幾次，聽到了甚麼？流言為甚麼還是傳開了？ How many times did Ge Pinlian listen at the door, and what did he hear? Why did the rumour still spread?
  2. 沈喻氏起疑，是因為看到了甚麼，還是聽到了甚麼？ Did Shen Yushi become suspicious because of something she saw, or something she heard?

Chapter 1

  1. 劉錫彤第一個錯是甚麼？請引原文。 What was Liu Xitong's first mistake? Quote the source.
  2. 葛畢氏的口供為甚麼不可靠？ Why is Ge Bishi's confession unreliable?
  3. 如果銀針依法擦洗，案子會不會不同？ If the silver needle had been washed as the manual says, would the case have gone differently?

Chapter 2

  1. 第一次京控為甚麼沒有用？ Why did the first appeal to the capital fail?
  2. 呈詞為甚麼不能把沒有旁證的事寫成確實的事？ Why should a petition not state an unsupported allegation as fact?

Chapter 3

  1. 胡瑞瀾訊出的供詞和原題不合，為甚麼還是照原擬奏結？ Hu Ruilan found testimony that did not match the original verdict. Why did he still send up a closing memorial with the original sentences?
  2. 「回護同官」是律例上的罪名嗎？它怎樣在本案中發生？ Was "shielding fellow officials" a legal offence? How did it happen in this case?

Chapter 4

  1. 如果沒有京控，這個案下一步會怎樣？ Without the appeals to the capital, what would have happened next?
  2. 選出冤案形成的第一個錯誤，並附上奏摺原文。 Choose the first error that made the wrongful conviction, and quote the memorial.

Epilogue

  1. 刑部說楊乃武「不知遠嫌」，說葛畢氏「不守婦道」；為甚麼對他只是責備，對她卻成了罪名？ The Board said Yang Naiwu "did not keep a proper distance" and Ge Bishi "did not keep a wife's proper conduct". Why was it only a rebuke for him, but the ground of punishment for her?
  2. 傳說為甚麼比檔案流傳得更廣？ Why did the legends spread further than the archive?

Each chapter's results screen also asks students to compare what they did with what the historical official did ("what is the same, and what is different?"). Their downloaded results are a good starting point for the discussion.

A gap to know about. None of these questions asks students to criticise the game itself as an account of the past — for example, what the five kinds of action leave out, or what it means that the two reconstructed documents and the imagined pictures sit next to real sources. If you want that discussion, add your own question.

If something goes wrong

  • The game will not load. Check the internet connection, then try another browser. Clear the browser's cache if an old version seems stuck.
  • A chapter's progress is gone. If the game content was updated, the game resets that chapter and says so. The learning record is kept. Students can use "Restart chapter" at any time; it asks before clearing.
  • The text is too small. Use the text-size setting in the game's Settings.

The Yang Naiwu Case (楊乃武案) — History Bible

Game built 25 September – 10 October 2026 · Published build of 27 September 2026 · Written for the gallery 13 October 2026

Who did the research

AI agents did the research, and the source project records this openly. GLM-5.3 searched for and downloaded the sources; Claude (the lead agent) divided the work, checked it and wrote the game; Codex reviewed the design and the code. The owner, Kwok-leong Tang, made the decisions about scope, audience and publishing. The work journal records no historian outside the project reviewing the content.

Two safeguards are built into the project:

  • Every quotation is checked by a script. The game has 163 quoted passages. Each one is copied by program from a downloaded copy of its source, and the build refuses a quotation that does not match that copy exactly. Each source file is fixed by a checksum (sha256), so a changed file is caught too.
  • Each chapter's history was checked by a second AI agent (GLM), separate from the agent that wrote it. Checks are recorded in research/qc/ of the source project.

These checks show that the quotations match the downloaded texts. They do not show that the downloaded texts are good editions (see "Known problems" below), and they are not a review by a historian.

How the game marks what is documented

The game labels every passage by its form, and the project counts them like this:

FormWhat it meansPassages
QuotationOriginal text from a source above, shown with its source. Students can switch it to a modern Chinese or an English version.155
ParaphraseThe game's own retelling of a source, with the source named ("Based on …").64
ReconstructionA scene, a line of dialogue or a document rebuilt from a source, where the original does not survive or was not collected. Marked "Reconstruction" with its basis.36
Game fictionText invented for play. The label exists, but no passage in the content files uses it.0

Historical statements carry a second label: established, recorded assertion (someone said it at the time; it may be false), disputed, or inference. There are 32 such statements in the game. Some of these labels stay hidden until the student has answered the question they would give away.

Things a historian should know about:

  • Two documents are reconstructed, and say so in their titles: "Shen Yushi's First Petition to the County (reconstructed)" and "Joint Report of Hangzhou Prefecture and Yuhang County (excerpt, reconstructed)" in chapter 4. Neither original was collected; both are rebuilt from the 1877 memorial's account of them.
  • One witness line is an inference. In chapter 4, one of Liu Xitong's statements is rebuilt from the memorial, which records no statement by him on that point. The game says this in the label.
  • The player's choices are counterfactual. Inside each chapter the student may act differently from the real official. The game then shows what the real official did, with the source, and the next chapter starts again from the real history. The chapter structure, the five kinds of action, and the grading are teaching models, not a reconstruction of how any Qing office worked day to day.
  • Background summaries and "Notes on the options" pages are written by the game.
  • All pictures are imagined. 29 portraits and scenes were made with AI image generation, and 4 paper textures were made for the game. The game says: "All pictures are imagined illustrations based on the documents. They are not historical sources. No portrait of anyone in this case can be verified." The owner approved all 33 pictures on 2026-10-10.
  • Popular stories appear only as popular stories. The epilogue sets some well-known legends about the case beside the archive and asks students to sort them. Those stories are labelled with their popular source.
  • The English is the project's own translation, made by AI agents. Every translation is still marked "draft" in the source project.

Known problems and open questions

These come from the source project's own notes. They are not settled.

About the texts

  1. The transcriptions are modern and imperfect. The Qing documents come from Wikisource, mostly in simplified characters, with OCR-type errors. The Shenbao reports are OCR transcriptions with missing characters. The research notes say the game's quotations should ideally be checked against photographic reprints of the Veritable Records and the Shenbao before formal use (R1, opening warning; research/README.md). This has not been done.
  2. The Code is the 1905 text, not the one used in 1873–1877. The game quotes Xue Yunsheng's Duli cunyi (Beijing printing of 1905). The articles were not checked one by one against a Tongzhi-era printing of the Code (R3 §12.2 item 2). The Kyoto website also says its corrections are not finished.
  3. No legal text was found for the rule that a provincial graduate (舉人) must lose his degree before he can be tortured. The case file shows the county asked to strip Yang Naiwu's degree first, and the heavy torture came at the prefecture. The game says the rule's text has not been found (R3 §12.2 item 3; design document, chapter 1).

About the facts

  1. Which office took the second appeal. The Board's memorial says the Gendarmerie (步軍統領衙門); the Peking Gazette copy of Guangshou's memorial says the Censorate. The game keeps both (R1 §4 item 3).
  2. The day the coffin was opened. Chinese Wikipedia and 五味知子's timeline both give 光緒二年十二月初九日 (1877-01-22). The primary documents do not record the day (R1 §4 item 13).
  3. The day of Hu Ruilan's closing memorial. The research note gives 十月初三; the primary documents do not record it (design document, chapter 3).
  4. The names of Hu Ruilan's deputies are spelled differently in the memorial, the Veritable Records and the edict (for example 邊葆城 / 邊葆諴 / 邊葆誠) (R1 §4 item 12).
  5. Ge Bishi's personal name. Official documents call her only 葛畢氏 or 畢氏. The name 畢秀姑 and the nickname "Little Cabbage" come from popular sources (R1 §4 item 10).
  6. Yang Naiwu's year of birth is uncertain: the 1874 petition implies about 1839; popular works often say 1841 (R1 §4 item 9).
  7. Whether there was an affair. The Board of Punishments found no proof and did not pursue it. The research note says the game should present it as a suspicion, not a fact (R1 §4 item 1).
  8. Shenbao reports are partly hearsay. Some vivid scenes in the Shenbao (for example the provincial judge's words to the prefect) are reports of what people said, and the project labels them so (R1 §4 item 6).

Books the project could not get

  1. The three most important modern works have no legal full text online, and the project did not use them: 陸永棣《落日殘照》(2018), 王策來《楊乃武與小白菜案真情披露》(2002), and 《浙江文史資料選輯》第52輯 (1993). The archive facsimile 《楊乃武小白菜奇案御檔》 (2003) could settle several small points. Weng Tonghe's diary was not available in full text (research/README.md).

Review status in the source project

The source project tracks a review status for each historical statement and each translation. As of 10 October 2026:

  • 22 of 32 historical statements are marked history_checked — checked by an AI agent (GLM) against the sources. 10 are still draft: the four in chapter 1 and six of Liu Xitong's statements in chapter 4. The game shows draft statements as "Not yet checked".
  • Every English translation is draft.
  • The project's own "release" check, which requires all of these to be approved, fails (883 items, journal entry of 2026-10-10). So the game can only be built at the project's development level, which allows draft text. The published game is such a build.

Before quoting the game's English, or one of its "Not yet checked" statements, as fact in class, check it against the source linked above.

Sources and rights

Built by AI agents for Kwok-leong Tang between 25 September and 10 October 2026: GLM-5.3 searched for and downloaded sources, Claude led and wrote the code, Codex reviewed the code and design. Almost every quotation comes from the Board of Punishments' memorial of 1877 (88 of 163 quoted passages); the rest are from edicts, the Veritable Records, the 1874 petition to the capital printed in the Shenbao, other Shenbao reports, the Great Qing Code and Collected Cases of Injustice Rectified. A script checks each quotation against the downloaded text. The texts are modern Wikisource transcriptions with some OCR errors, and the Code text is a 1905 edition, not the one in force in 1873–1877. Two documents are reconstructed and labelled as such, one witness line is labelled an inference, and the player's choices inside each chapter are counterfactual: each chapter returns to the real history at the end. All pictures are imagined. The English is a draft translation. No person has reviewed the history.

The game quotes Qing official documents (the Board of Punishments memorial and the edicts of 1877, the Veritable Records of the Guangxu Emperor, a Censorate memorial of 1874), Shenbao reports of 1874–1875, the Great Qing Code, Collected Cases of Injustice Rectified (洗冤集錄) and Wang Huizu, all through Wikisource transcriptions that the source project records as public domain. Code articles also come from Kyoto University's electronic text of Xue Yunsheng's Duli cunyi; the project records the old text as public domain and the electronic edition as Kyoto University scholarly material. The epilogue quotes a 2013 newspaper article (short quotation only). The Chinese Wikipedia article (CC BY-SA 4.0), a modern novel and Alford's 1984 article are used as a basis, not quoted. The 33 pictures are AI-generated or made for the game, and are labelled in the game as imagined, not historical sources. Fonts: Noto Serif TC and Noto Sans TC, SIL Open Font License 1.1. The author has not stated a licence for the game itself. Licence: Not stated.
