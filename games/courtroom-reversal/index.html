Courtroom Reversal

As a fictional modern lawyer, students investigate the Yang Naiwu and Xiaobaicai case (1873–1877) in three short chapters. They collect records from the Board of Punishments' 1877 memorial, press testimony, and present evidence at the statement it contradicts. Then they choose a closing assessment that says no more than the record supports. It makes them argue about whether matching or repeated statements can count as proof.

Courtroom visual novel: investigation, pressing testimony, presenting evidence 45 minutes One player per device; a pair can share one screen Undergraduate (suggested; not tried with students) Contributed by Kwok-leong Tang

Courtroom Reversal (逆轉公堂) — Teacher Guide

Historical bilingual edition (pilot) · Three chapters and a closing assessment · About 20–30 minutes of play (the game's own estimate, not timed) · 繁體中文 and English

The gallery listing gives 45 minutes: the game's upper estimate of 30 minutes, plus about 15 minutes for a short briefing and a debrief. Nobody has timed a class yet. Time one full play-through yourself before you plan around it.

The learning task

The case is the Yang Naiwu and Xiaobaicai case (楊乃武與小白菜案). In Yuhang, Zhejiang, in 1873, Ge Pinlian died. His wife, Ge Bishi (known by the later popular name Xiaobaicai), and the scholar Yang Naiwu were accused of poisoning him. Both confessed under torture, and the provincial courts proposed death sentences. Years of appeals followed. In the end the Board of Punishments (刑部) in Beijing had the remains re-examined, found no poison, and settled the case in a memorial of 1877.

The game is about one question that runs through the whole case: when several statements agree, or a confession is repeated, is that proof? Students learn to ask where a statement came from, whether a report matches what was actually done, and under what conditions a confession was made.

All the evidence comes from one document: the Board of Punishments' own memorial of 1877, which settled the case. Students read short quotations from it in the original Chinese, with an explanation in Traditional Chinese or English.

This is not a reconstruction of a real hearing. The lawyer, Cheng Yilong (成一龍), is a modern, fictional character. All spoken lines are invented, and the game says so on screen. The real review took years and was done by officials, not by a lawyer in one courtroom. The game tells students this at the end.

What students do

Each chapter has the same two parts.

1. Investigation. Students visit three places. At each one they read a short conversation and collect one record (a 案證) for their evidence bag. They must visit all three before the hearing opens. The Evidence button shows every record collected. Each record has an Inspect source button that shows the original Chinese quotation from the memorial and an explanation.

2. Hearing. A witness (or, in chapter 2, the presiding official reading from the file) gives three statements. Students can:

  • move between statements with Previous and Next;
  • Press a statement to hear a follow-up. Pressing is free and often gives a hint;
  • Present a record against the statement on screen.

Exactly one record contradicts exactly one statement. If students present the wrong record, or the right record at the wrong statement, they lose 20 points of Court standing and get a short explanation of why it did not work. Standing starts at 100. At zero the hearing is adjourned, and they replay the chapter. Standing goes back to 100 at the start of each new chapter.

After the right objection, a short screen explains what the objection does and does not establish. These notes matter. For example, chapter 1 ends by saying that undermining one statement does not decide the cause of death. Ask students to read them, not click past them.

The three chapters

The chapters do not follow the order of events. They follow the questions backward through the file, from the latest evidence to the earliest. The game says this on screen.

  1. One statement. Two origins (一紙供詞，兩種來歷). The pharmacist Qian Baosheng signed a statement (甘結) that matches Yang Naiwu's confession. Students trace how that statement was made, and ask whether two matching accounts are really two independent sources.
  2. The disputed silver needle (銀針上的疑點). Students compare what the memorial says was done at the first examination of the body with what was later reported to superiors. This is about a contradiction between records. It does not teach that silver-needle testing works; the game says this clearly.
  3. After confession, before truth (招認之後，真相之前). Students look at the first petition, the conditions under which Ge Bishi first confessed, and a later review that produced the same confessions again. The question is whether repeating a confession shows that it was given freely.

The closing assessment

After chapter 3, students choose one of three closing assessments. The wrong choices give specific feedback and can be tried again with no loss of standing. The right choice is the one that says only what the evidence supports. The final screen then:

  • reports the Board's finding (no poison; death by illness), and says this is a historical finding, not a modern medical diagnosis;
  • shows, in a section called The limits of vindication (平反的限度), that the memorial still blamed Ge Bishi for her conduct and proposed punishment for her, and did not restore Yang's examination degree.

This ending is deliberate. The game does not give a simple happy ending. It is a good place to start the debrief.

Two languages

Choose 繁體中文 or English in the header, at any time. Switching keeps the current line, evidence, standing, and statement. The quotations from the memorial always stay in their original simplified characters, in both language versions. Only the explanations change.

This makes the game usable for:

  • a Chinese-reading class, which reads the original quotations directly;
  • an English-only class, which relies on the explanations and can still see what the source looks like;
  • a mixed class, where pairs can switch languages and compare the explanation with the quotation.

The English translations and the Traditional Chinese explanations were written for the game. They are not part of the memorial. Tell students this.

Saving, resuming, and starting over

  • The game saves by itself at checkpoints: during investigation, at the hearing, and at the end of each chapter. If a student closes the page, they choose Resume case (接續卷宗) when they return. A conversation that was not finished may play again from the start.
  • Saves stay in that browser on that device only. They do not move to another computer, and there is no file to export.
  • Start new case (新開卷宗) on the opening screen replaces the saved game. Replay chapter restarts the current chapter and keeps the records from earlier chapters. To get back to the opening screen, reload the page.
  • If the browser blocks saving (for example in some private windows), the game still works for that session and shows "Saving unavailable".
  • Shared classroom computers: the next student will see the last student's Resume case. Tell students to press Start new case if they want a fresh game.

Running it in class

Before class.

  • Open the game's web address on the classroom computer and on one student device. Play one full chapter. Check that the text is readable on the projector.
  • Students need the internet to open the game from the gallery's link. If your room has poor internet, ask the author for the game folder: the source project says the folder also runs when opened from disk. It is a folder (an index.html file and an assets folder), not a single file, so students need the whole folder.
  • Sound is off by default. You do not need headphones.
  • The game mentions torture. It does not describe it in detail. It also mentions a death and an accusation of adultery. Warn students if this matters for your class.

Suggested debrief questions

These questions are suggestions written by an agent from the game's own content. The game itself does not ask them. No instructor has used them yet. Change them to suit your class.

  1. In all three chapters, something looked like proof because it was repeated or because two records agreed. Choose one case. What made it look strong, and what made it weak?
  2. Every record in the game comes from one document, written by the office that reversed the verdict. What might that office want readers to believe? What can this document not tell us?
  3. The game says the people at the time did not have the whole file in front of them, as you did. Is it fair to judge the county magistrate by what you could see?
  4. The game gives Ge Bishi and Yang Naiwu spoken lines that they never said, and labels them as reconstructions. Is that acceptable in a history lesson? Where would you draw the line?
  5. The game tells the story through a modern lawyer and a single courtroom. What does this format make easy to see, and what does it hide about how a Qing case was actually reviewed?
  6. The final screen says the memorial still blamed Ge Bishi and proposed punishment for her. Does the word "reversal" in the title describe what happened to her? Why might the game end this way instead of with a happy ending?

Cautions

  • One source. The game rests on one official memorial, through a Wikisource transcription that has not been checked against an archival copy. It is a good tool for source criticism, not a full history of the case.
  • Silver needle. Chapter 2 is about records that contradict each other. Make sure students do not leave thinking silver needles detect poison.
  • No new culprit. One wrong closing choice names the magistrate's son as the poisoner. That comes from a novel, not from the record. The game explains why it is wrong.
  • Not tested for access. Nobody has played the game by keyboard alone or with a screen reader. See test-ledger.md.

Changing the content

The game is not stored in this gallery folder. To change it, you or your agent need the source project. All text is in two files: assets/pilot-content.js (chapter 1, the interface, and the glossary) and assets/campaign-content.js (chapters 2 and 3, and the closing assessment). Every text comes as a pair, Traditional Chinese and English, so change both. After any change, run node tests/content.test.cjs in the source project. It checks that every text has both languages and that every quotation still appears word for word in the local copy of the memorial. Do not add a quotation that is not in the memorial.

Courtroom Reversal (逆轉公堂) — History Bible

Purpose

The game teaches one habit of source criticism: agreement and repetition are not the same as proof. It uses the Yang Naiwu and Xiaobaicai case (楊乃武與小白菜案), 1873–1877, because the official record of that case shows, step by step, how one unsafe statement supported the next. The courtroom, the lawyer, and the dialogue are a teaching frame, not a reconstruction of any real hearing.

In English the game calls it the "Board of Punishments case-settlement memorial, Guangxu 3/2 (1877)". The transcription opens with 光绪三年二月刑部尚书皂保等奏, so the memorial is presented by the Board under its president Zaobao (皂保) and others. The year 1877 is the repository's own conversion of Guangxu 3; this listing did not check the exact Western date of the second month.

The local copy is assets/sources/memorial-1877.txt. The game also ships a reading page, assets/sources/memorial-1877.html, built from that text by scripts/build_source.py. Students can open it from the Sources & glossary screen without the internet. The online Wikisource link needs the internet.

What kind of source it is. It is an official, retrospective account written by the office that reversed the verdict. It is not a transcript of any hearing. It has its own point of view: for example, it says the earlier officials did not act from malice (先后承审各员尚非故勘故入). The game says this kind of thing plainly: "The official closing account also has its own perspective."

What has not been checked. The Wikisource transcription has not been compared with an archival copy of the memorial. The full text keeps apparent errors, punctuation, and character forms. For example, it spells the attendant's name both 沈采泉 and 沈彩泉.

What remains from the novel-based original

The main branch of the same repository holds an earlier game, made in May 2026, based on a late Qing novel about the case. Its notice links to the Chinese Text Project (中國哲學書電子化計劃, https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?if=gb&res=973161) as the source of the story. The repository also contains a novel text, ylmyxbc.txt, whose preface names two novels (by 黃南丁氏 and by 靈岩樵子). This listing did not check whether that file is the same text as the ctext page: unverified.

Kept in this edition:

  • The courtroom background (assets/courtroom-key-art.png) and the character sprite sheets in assets/characters/. The old notice says the characters, script, and art were "adapted and generated by this project" (本專案改編與生成). The repository does not say which tool made the images, or under what terms. This edition uses five of the sheets (Cheng Yilong, the presiding official, Yang Naiwu, Qian Baosheng, Ge Bishi). Two others (liu-sheet.png, sangu-sheet.png) are still in the folder but are not shown.
  • The fictional lawyer Cheng Yilong.
  • The game mechanics: investigation, pressing, presenting evidence, and court standing.
  • The colour palette and much of the page styling.

Dropped in this edition:

  • The novel's plot, including the magistrate's son as the real murderer and a forged pharmacy ledger. One wrong closing choice names the magistrate's son, so that the game can explain why a novel's plot cannot fill a gap in the evidence.
  • The novel text as a source. ylmyxbc.txt is still in the repository, but the game does not load it and the packaging script does not copy it.
  • The old link to ctext.org, and the old page's Google Analytics script. The source project's test checks that the analytics script is gone.

Sensitive content

The game mentions torture, a death, an accusation of adultery, and the memorial's judgment of Ge Bishi's conduct. It does not describe violence in detail. The final screen points students to the gender and moral judgments inside the official finding.

For anyone editing the game

Keep the rule the source project follows: every Chinese quotation must appear word for word in assets/sources/memorial-1877.txt, and the test will fail if it does not. If the transcription itself changes, rebuild the reading page with scripts/build_source.py and update the hash in provenance.json. New background facts that do not come from the memorial must be labelled in the game, and added to the ledger above as unverified until checked.

How to re-run the tests

In the source repository (https://github.com/kltng/yzgt-codex, branch codex/historical-bilingual-pilot):

``sh node tests/content.test.cjs ``

This needs only Node. It reads files and changes nothing.

The browser test needs Playwright. The source README gives these steps:

``sh python3 -m venv .venv .venv/bin/pip install -r tests/requirements.txt .venv/bin/python -m playwright install chromium .venv/bin/python tests/playthrough.py ``

It rebuilds dist/, writes screenshots to tmp/qa/, and prints a PASS line at the end.

The checker reads a file; it cannot play a game or stand in a classroom. These six checks need someone to actually do them. This is what the contributor reported, in their words. Where they said who did the testing, the status says so: a check run by an agent or a script is not the same as a person trying it. A check they did not report is shown as untested.

Sources and rights

Built on one source: the Board of Punishments' 1877 case-settlement memorial on the Yang Naiwu and Xiaobaicai case, from a Wikisource transcription brought over from the author's qing_court_game research collection. Twelve short Chinese excerpts are quoted in their original simplified characters and are checked by a test against a local copy of that transcription. The English translations and Traditional Chinese explanations were written for this game. The lawyer Cheng Yilong, all spoken dialogue, the three compressed hearings, and the player's access to later records are invented teaching devices, and the game labels them as such. The art and the lawyer character come from an earlier version of the game based on a late Qing novel; that version's plot (the magistrate's son as murderer, a forged pharmacy ledger) is not used.

The main source is the Board of Punishments' case-settlement memorial of Guangxu 3/2 (1877), a Qing government text in the public domain. The game uses the simplified-character Wikisource transcription, archived on 2026-09-25; it may contain errors and has not been checked against an archival facsimile. The courtroom background and the character sprite sheets come from the earlier, novel-based version of this game. That version's notice calls its characters, script and art 'adapted and generated by this project' (本專案改編與生成) and limits use to non-commercial teaching, research and display. The source repository names no licence for the code or the art, and this edition does not add one. The sound cues are made in the browser; there are no recordings. Licence: Not stated.
