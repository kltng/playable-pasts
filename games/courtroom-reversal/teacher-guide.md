# Courtroom Reversal (逆轉公堂) — Teacher Guide

Historical bilingual edition (pilot) · Three chapters and a closing assessment · About 20–30
minutes of play (the game's own estimate, not timed) · 繁體中文 and English

The gallery listing gives 45 minutes: the game's upper estimate of 30 minutes, plus about 15
minutes for a short briefing and a debrief. Nobody has timed a class yet. Time one full
play-through yourself before you plan around it.

## The learning task

The case is the Yang Naiwu and Xiaobaicai case (楊乃武與小白菜案). In Yuhang, Zhejiang, in
1873, Ge Pinlian died. His wife, Ge Bishi (known by the later popular name Xiaobaicai), and
the scholar Yang Naiwu were accused of poisoning him. Both confessed under torture, and the
provincial courts proposed death sentences. Years of appeals followed. In the end the Board of
Punishments (刑部) in Beijing had the remains re-examined, found no poison, and settled the case
in a memorial of 1877.

The game is about one question that runs through the whole case: **when several statements
agree, or a confession is repeated, is that proof?** Students learn to ask where a statement
came from, whether a report matches what was actually done, and under what conditions a
confession was made.

All the evidence comes from one document: the Board of Punishments' own memorial of 1877, which
settled the case. Students read short quotations from it in the original Chinese, with an
explanation in Traditional Chinese or English.

This is not a reconstruction of a real hearing. The lawyer, Cheng Yilong (成一龍), is a modern,
fictional character. All spoken lines are invented, and the game says so on screen. The real
review took years and was done by officials, not by a lawyer in one courtroom. The game tells
students this at the end.

## What students do

Each chapter has the same two parts.

**1. Investigation.** Students visit three places. At each one they read a short conversation
and collect one record (a 案證) for their evidence bag. They must visit all three before the
hearing opens. The **Evidence** button shows every record collected. Each record has an
**Inspect source** button that shows the original Chinese quotation from the memorial and an
explanation.

**2. Hearing.** A witness (or, in chapter 2, the presiding official reading from the file)
gives three statements. Students can:

- move between statements with **Previous** and **Next**;
- **Press** a statement to hear a follow-up. Pressing is free and often gives a hint;
- **Present** a record against the statement on screen.

Exactly one record contradicts exactly one statement. If students present the wrong record, or
the right record at the wrong statement, they lose 20 points of **Court standing** and get a
short explanation of why it did not work. Standing starts at 100. At zero the hearing is
adjourned, and they replay the chapter. Standing goes back to 100 at the start of each new
chapter.

After the right objection, a short screen explains what the objection **does** and **does
not** establish. These notes matter. For example, chapter 1 ends by saying that undermining one
statement does not decide the cause of death. Ask students to read them, not click past them.

## The three chapters

The chapters do not follow the order of events. They follow the questions backward through the
file, from the latest evidence to the earliest. The game says this on screen.

1. **One statement. Two origins** (一紙供詞，兩種來歷). The pharmacist Qian Baosheng signed a
   statement (甘結) that matches Yang Naiwu's confession. Students trace how that statement was
   made, and ask whether two matching accounts are really two independent sources.
2. **The disputed silver needle** (銀針上的疑點). Students compare what the memorial says was
   done at the first examination of the body with what was later reported to superiors. This
   is about a contradiction between records. It does **not** teach that silver-needle testing
   works; the game says this clearly.
3. **After confession, before truth** (招認之後，真相之前). Students look at the first
   petition, the conditions under which Ge Bishi first confessed, and a later review that
   produced the same confessions again. The question is whether repeating a confession shows
   that it was given freely.

## The closing assessment

After chapter 3, students choose one of three closing assessments. The wrong choices give
specific feedback and can be tried again with no loss of standing. The right choice is the one
that says only what the evidence supports. The final screen then:

- reports the Board's finding (no poison; death by illness), and says this is a historical
  finding, not a modern medical diagnosis;
- shows, in a section called **The limits of vindication** (平反的限度), that the memorial still
  blamed Ge Bishi for her conduct and proposed punishment for her, and did not restore Yang's
  examination degree.

This ending is deliberate. The game does not give a simple happy ending. It is a good place to
start the debrief.

## Two languages

Choose 繁體中文 or English in the header, at any time. Switching keeps the current line,
evidence, standing, and statement. The quotations from the memorial always stay in their
original simplified characters, in both language versions. Only the explanations change.

This makes the game usable for:

- a Chinese-reading class, which reads the original quotations directly;
- an English-only class, which relies on the explanations and can still see what the source
  looks like;
- a mixed class, where pairs can switch languages and compare the explanation with the
  quotation.

The English translations and the Traditional Chinese explanations were written for the game.
They are not part of the memorial. Tell students this.

## Saving, resuming, and starting over

- The game saves by itself at checkpoints: during investigation, at the hearing, and at the end
  of each chapter. If a student closes the page, they choose **Resume case** (接續卷宗) when they
  return. A conversation that was not finished may play again from the start.
- Saves stay in **that browser on that device only**. They do not move to another computer, and
  there is no file to export.
- **Start new case** (新開卷宗) on the opening screen replaces the saved game. **Replay
  chapter** restarts the current chapter and keeps the records from earlier chapters. To get
  back to the opening screen, reload the page.
- If the browser blocks saving (for example in some private windows), the game still works for
  that session and shows "Saving unavailable".
- **Shared classroom computers:** the next student will see the last student's **Resume case**.
  Tell students to press **Start new case** if they want a fresh game.

## Running it in class

**Before class.**

- Open the game's web address on the classroom computer and on one student device. Play one
  full chapter. Check that the text is readable on the projector.
- Students need the internet to open the game from the gallery's link. If your room has poor
  internet, ask the author for the game folder: the source project says the folder also runs
  when opened from disk. It is a folder (an `index.html` file and an `assets` folder), not a
  single file, so students need the whole folder.
- Sound is off by default. You do not need headphones.
- The game mentions torture. It does not describe it in detail. It also mentions a death and an
  accusation of adultery. Warn students if this matters for your class.

**A 45-minute plan (suggested, not yet tried).**

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Briefing. Say in two sentences what the case was. Say that Cheng Yilong and all dialogue are invented, and that the quotations come from a real 1877 document. Choose the language. |
| 5–15 | Chapter 1. Ask students to press every statement before they present anything. |
| 15–23 | Chapter 2. |
| 23–32 | Chapter 3 and the closing assessment. |
| 32–35 | Students open **Sources & glossary** (史料與詞彙) and read the source note and one quotation. |
| 35–45 | Debrief. |

Students work alone or in pairs on one screen. In a pair, one student reads the quotations and
the other reads the explanations, and they must agree before presenting evidence.

If you have less time, play chapter 1 only (about 10 minutes) and use questions 1 and 2 below.

## Suggested debrief questions

These questions are **suggestions written by an agent** from the game's own content. The game
itself does not ask them. No instructor has used them yet. Change them to suit your class.

1. In all three chapters, something looked like proof because it was repeated or because two
   records agreed. Choose one case. What made it look strong, and what made it weak?
2. Every record in the game comes from one document, written by the office that reversed the
   verdict. What might that office want readers to believe? What can this document not tell us?
3. The game says the people at the time did not have the whole file in front of them, as you
   did. Is it fair to judge the county magistrate by what you could see?
4. The game gives Ge Bishi and Yang Naiwu spoken lines that they never said, and labels them as
   reconstructions. Is that acceptable in a history lesson? Where would you draw the line?
5. The game tells the story through a modern lawyer and a single courtroom. What does this
   format make easy to see, and what does it hide about how a Qing case was actually reviewed?
6. The final screen says the memorial still blamed Ge Bishi and proposed punishment for her.
   Does the word "reversal" in the title describe what happened to her? Why might the game
   end this way instead of with a happy ending?

## Cautions

- **One source.** The game rests on one official memorial, through a Wikisource transcription
  that has not been checked against an archival copy. It is a good tool for source criticism,
  not a full history of the case.
- **Silver needle.** Chapter 2 is about records that contradict each other. Make sure students
  do not leave thinking silver needles detect poison.
- **No new culprit.** One wrong closing choice names the magistrate's son as the poisoner. That
  comes from a novel, not from the record. The game explains why it is wrong.
- **Not tested for access.** Nobody has played the game by keyboard alone or with a screen
  reader. See `test-ledger.md`.

## Changing the content

The game is not stored in this gallery folder. To change it, you or your agent need the source
project. All text is in two files: `assets/pilot-content.js` (chapter 1, the interface, and the
glossary) and `assets/campaign-content.js` (chapters 2 and 3, and the closing assessment). Every
text comes as a pair, Traditional Chinese and English, so change both. After any change, run
`node tests/content.test.cjs` in the source project. It checks that every text has both
languages and that every quotation still appears word for word in the local copy of the
memorial. Do not add a quotation that is not in the memorial.
